How old is my dog in human years?

The multiply-by-seven rule is a myth. Here is what the research actually says, with full tables by breed size.

A one-year-old dog is about 31 in human years — not seven.

After that they age roughly four human years for every one of ours, though it slows down as they get older and it depends heavily on their size.

The "multiply by seven" rule has no scientific basis. It appears to have started as a marketing line in the 1950s to encourage owners to book annual check-ups, and it has been repeated ever since because it is easy to do in your head.

It also produces obvious nonsense. It suggests a one-year-old dog is equivalent to a seven-year-old child — but a one-year-old dog can reproduce, and a seven-year-old child cannot. It also treats a chihuahua and a Great Dane identically, when one routinely lives to sixteen and the other rarely reaches nine.

What the research actually says

In 2020, researchers at the University of California San Diego published a study in Cell Systems comparing DNA methylation — chemical marks that accumulate on DNA in predictable patterns as an animal ages — in 104 Labrador retrievers against 320 humans. This is known as an epigenetic clock, and it is the same technique used to estimate biological age in people.

Their result was a logarithmic relationship rather than a straight line:

human age = 16 × ln(dog age) + 31

The practical meaning is that dogs do most of their ageing early. A dog covers roughly three decades of human-equivalent development in its first year, then slows down considerably. That matches what owners actually observe: the difference between a puppy at three months and one at twelve months is enormous, while the difference between a dog at eight and one at nine is small.

When is a dog considered senior?

Not at a fixed age. A giant breed can be senior by six; a small terrier may not be until eleven. The American Animal Hospital Association defines life stages proportionally to expected lifespan rather than by calendar age, which is why a vet may call your Great Dane senior at an age when your neighbour's chihuahua is still middle-aged.

Life stage matters more than the number, because it is what changes clinical advice: senior dogs are generally recommended twice-yearly examinations rather than annual ones.

Why this is worth knowing

A dog passes about sixty birthdays on their own clock across a life of roughly five thousand days. Almost all of them go by unmarked, because they do not land on any calendar you own. Knowing where your dog actually is — rather than a rough multiple — tells you when to expect changes, when to raise things with a vet, and when there is something worth making a fuss about.

Common questions

How old is a 1 year old dog in human years?

About 31. The 2020 UC San Diego epigenetic clock gives 16 × ln(1) + 31, which is exactly 31. A one-year-old dog is a young adult, not a child — which is also why they are sexually mature.

How old is a 7 year old dog in human years?

Roughly 62 for a medium breed. For a giant breed it is closer to 70, and for a small breed closer to 55. Seven is commonly where vets begin using the word senior for larger dogs.

Is the 7 year rule wrong?

Yes. It has no scientific basis and appears to have originated as a mid-century marketing device to encourage annual veterinary visits. It also ignores breed size, which is the second largest factor after age.

Do big dogs really age faster?

Yes, and substantially. A Great Dane has a median lifespan around eight years while a chihuahua often reaches sixteen. At five years old the Great Dane is roughly eleven human years older than the chihuahua.

How do I calculate my dog's age if they are a rescue?

Use your vet's best estimate and treat the result as approximate. An error of a month or two barely moves the number, and after the first year the curve flattens enough that small uncertainties matter very little.
